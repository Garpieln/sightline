Improvisation is composition in real time. It gets easier the smaller you make your choices at first.

A progression of constraints

  1. Pentatonic only (C D E G A over C major chords). No wrong notes.
  2. Chord tones on beat 1. Land on a note of the current chord at each chord change. Fill between them freely.
  3. One rhythm, many notes. Choose one rhythm (for example, long-short-short) and repeat it with different pitches.
  4. Call and response. Play a 2-bar idea, then answer it with a variation.
  5. Develop a motif: repeat it, move it up a step (sequence), turn it upside down (inversion), stretch its rhythm.

Scales for different moods

  • Major pentatonic: open, folk, country, pop.
  • Minor pentatonic (A C D E G): rock, soul.
  • Blues scale (A C D E♭ E G): minor pentatonic plus the "blue note."
  • Dorian: funk, jazz, modal minor.
  • Mixolydian: rock, blues-tinged major.

OpinionSilence is the most underused tool for beginner improvisers. Leaving gaps makes ideas sound deliberate.
