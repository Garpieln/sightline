Fluent readers don't decode letter by letter. They recognize landmarks and shapes: how far the next note is, and which way it moves.

Landmark notes

LandmarkWhere
Middle C (C4)One ledger line below the treble staff
Treble G (G4)Second line from the bottom, where the treble clef curls
Treble C (C5)Third space from the bottom
High G (G5)Space just above the top line
High C (C6)Two ledger lines above the staff

Read by interval

  • Step (2nd): line to the next space, or space to the next line.
  • Skip (3rd): line to the next line, or space to the next space.
  • 4th / 5th: line to space / line to line with one line skipped.

Find the first note by landmark, then read everything after it as a step or skip up or down. That's what Read & play trains.

Reading ahead

EvidenceSkilled sight-readers look further ahead of what they're playing (a larger eye-hand span), and read in groups rather than single notes. Train this by keeping your eyes just ahead of the moving band.

Try itIn Read & play, choose Wait mode, Octave range. Before you play each note, say "step up," "skip down," and so on out loud. Then switch to Tempo mode at 50 bpm.
