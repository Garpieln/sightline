A reliable process

  1. Choose a mood and key. Write a one-line intent: "hopeful, slow, building."
  2. Write a 4-chord progression for the main section.
  3. Find a motif: a 2–4 note idea with a clear rhythm. Improvise over the chords until one sticks.
  4. Build a phrase: motif → repeat or vary → contrast → cadence. A common shape is a sentence: idea, idea varied, then a longer phrase driving to the cadence.
  5. Write a contrasting section. Change at least two of: key/mode, register, rhythm, texture, chords.
  6. Arrange a form and return to familiar material at the end.

Melody guidelines

  • ConventionMostly steps, with occasional leaps. After a big leap, step back in the opposite direction.
  • Give the melody a high point, ideally once and late in the phrase.
  • Put chord tones on strong beats, passing tones on weak beats.
  • Repeat enough to be memorable. Vary enough to stay interesting.
