A major scale follows the pattern W–W–H–W–W–W–H (W = whole step, H = half step). Start that pattern on any note and you get that note's major scale.

Circle of fifths

Moving up a 5th adds one sharp; moving down a 5th (up a 4th) adds one flat.

Minor scales

  • Natural minor: W–H–W–W–H–W–W. Same notes as its relative major, starting on the 6th degree.
  • Harmonic minor: raise the 7th. Creates the strong leading tone that pulls to the tonic.
  • Melodic minor: raise the 6th and 7th going up; classical usage lowers them coming down.

Modes

Start a major scale on a different degree and you get a mode with its own color: Dorian (minor with a bright 6th), Phrygian (dark, Spanish-sounding ♭2), Lydian (dreamy ♯4), Mixolydian (major with a bluesy ♭7), Aeolian (natural minor), Locrian (unstable).

Try itPlay all the white keys from D to D. That's D Dorian. Hold a D minor chord in the left hand and improvise on the white keys. Compare it with A to A over A minor.
