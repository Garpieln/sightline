A method for writing your own

  1. Start and end on I (or vi for a minor feel).
  2. Move by function: tonic → predominant (ii, IV) → dominant (V) → tonic.
  3. Use root motion as a guide. Roots falling by 5ths (vi–ii–V–I) sound strong and inevitable. Roots moving by 3rds (I–vi–IV) sound smooth. Roots moving up by step (IV–V–vi) sound like building energy.
  4. Add color: 7ths, sus chords, then borrowed chords.

Color chords

  • Borrowed iv: F minor in C major (I–IV–iv–I). Bittersweet.
  • ♭VI and ♭VII: A♭ and B♭ in C major. Big, cinematic, often heard as ♭VI–♭VII–I.
  • Secondary dominant: the V of a chord other than I. E7 → Am is "V of vi" and gives a strong pull.
Try itLoop C–G–Am–F. Then replace F with Fm on the fourth time through. Then end with A♭–B♭–C. Listen to how each change shifts the mood.
