Ear playing is pattern recognition: knowing what scale degrees and chord functions sound like, then finding them on the keys.

Steps for figuring out a song

  1. Find home. Hum the note that feels like the song's resting point. Find it on the keyboard. That's probably the key's tonic.
  2. Find the bass line. Bass notes usually spell the chord roots. Hum along and match on the piano.
  3. Decide chord quality. Try major and minor on each bass note. In a major key, most chords fit I, IV, V, vi, ii.
  4. Find the melody by interval. Start from a known note, then ask: step or leap? up or down?
  5. Check against function. If the phrase feels finished, it likely ended on I. If it feels hanging, it likely ended on V.

Skills to drill

  • Interval recognition (use the song cues in the Intervals lesson).
  • Major vs. minor chord quality.
  • Singing scale degrees with solfège: do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, ti.
  • Recognizing I, IV, V and vi by sound in a looped progression.

EvidenceSinging what you hear before playing it strengthens the link between your inner hearing and the keyboard.

Try itPick a simple song you know well, like "Twinkle Twinkle" or "Happy Birthday." Start on C and find the melody using only your ear. Then add a left-hand chord under each bar from C, F and G.
