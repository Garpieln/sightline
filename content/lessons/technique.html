Tension is the main obstacle to speed and the main cause of playing injuries. Build relaxed habits now. They're far harder to fix later.

Setup

  • Bench height: forearms roughly level with the keys, elbows slightly above the keyboard.
  • Distance: elbows a little in front of your torso. You should be able to lean slightly forward.
  • Hand shape: fingers naturally curved, like resting your hand on a ball. Play on the fingertip pads, with the thumb on its side edge.
  • Wrist: level and loose. It moves to help the fingers, especially for thumb crossings.

Movement

  • ConventionArm weight, not finger pressing. Let the arm's weight transfer into the key, then release. Pressing down after the note sounds does nothing on a piano.
  • ConventionConsistent fingering. Pick a fingering and use the same one every time. Changing it makes the motor program unstable.
  • Thumb under, finger over. In scales the thumb passes under the hand; going down, finger 3 or 4 crosses over the thumb. Keep the elbow moving with the hand.
  • Slow is the default. Play at a tempo where you can stay relaxed and accurate. Speed comes from fluency.
Try itPlay C–D–E–F–G with the right hand at 60 bpm. After each note, check your shoulder, wrist and thumb for tension. If any are tight, stop, shake out, restart slower.
